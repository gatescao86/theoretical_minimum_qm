# Exercise 3.1 — Hermitian eigenvectors form a basis

**Status.** Not started  
**Section.** 3.1

## Problem

Prove the following: If a vector space is N-dimensional, an orthonormal basis of N vectors can be constructed from the eigenvectors of a Hermitian operator.

## Solution

We prove by induction. 

**Base case:** N = 1. The statement is trivial, since a Hermitian operator on a 1D space has one eigenvector, and after normalization that one eigenvector forms the orthonormal basis.

**Induction hypothesis:**
Assume that for every Hermitian operator acting on an (N-1)-dimensional vector space, an orthonormal basis of N-1 vectors can be constructed from the eigenvectors of a Hermitian operator.

Let $H|u_1\rangle = \lambda_1|u_1\rangle$

Define 

$$W = \{|v\rangle \in V: \langle u_1|v\rangle = 0 \}$$

Then, 

$$\dim W = N-1$$

$$\langle u_1|H|v\rangle = （H |u_1 \rangle)^\dagger|v\rangle = (\lambda_1 |u_1 \rangle)^\dagger|v\rangle = \lambda_1^*\langle u_1|v \rangle$$ 

Since H is Hermitian, $\lambda_1$ is real, so $\lambda_1^* = \lambda_1$

Therefore,

$$\langle u_1|H|v\rangle = \lambda_1 \langle u_1|v\rangle = 0$$

So $H|v\rangle \in W$

By the induction hypothesis, $W$ has an orthonormal eigenbasis:

$$|u_2\rangle ... |u_N\rangle $$

Therefore, 

$$|u_1\rangle, |u_2\rangle ... |u_N\rangle$$
