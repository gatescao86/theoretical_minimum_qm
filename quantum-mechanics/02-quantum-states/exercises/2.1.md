# Exercise 2.1 — Orthogonality of $\lvert r\rangle$ and $\lvert l\rangle$

**Status.** Not started  
**Section.** 2.3

## Problem
Prove that the vector $|r\rangle$ is orthogonal to vector $|l\rangle$ where 
$|r\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|u\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|d\rangle$, 
$|l\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|u\rangle - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|d\rangle$



## Solution
To prove $|r\rangle$ is orthogonal to vector $|l\rangle$, we need to show $\langle l|r\rangle = 0$

$$
\langle l|r\rangle = (\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\langle u| - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\langle d|) (\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|u\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|d\rangle) 
= \frac{1}{2} \langle u|u \rangle +  \frac{1}{2} \langle u|d \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \langle d|u \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \langle d|d \rangle = 0
$$
