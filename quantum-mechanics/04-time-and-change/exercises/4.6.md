# Exercise 4.6 — Schrödinger-ket recipe for a single spin

**Status.** Not started  
**Section.** 4.13

## Problem

Carry out the Schrödinger Ket receipe for a single spin. The Hamiltonian is $H = \frac{\omega \hbar}{2} \sigma_z$ and the final observable is $\sigma_x$. The initial state is given as $|u \rangle$ (the state in which $\sigma_z$ = +1)

After time t, an experiment is done to measure $\sigma_y$. What are the possible outcomes and what are the probabilities for those outcomes? 

## Solution

For $H = \frac{\omega \hbar}{2} \sigma_z$, the eigenvalues are $E_j = \pm \frac{\omega \hbar}{2}$ 

$\sigma_z |u \rangle = +|u \rangle$ and $\sigma_z |d \rangle = -|d \rangle$, therefore

$$
H |u \rangle = +\frac{\omega \hbar}{2}|u \rangle
,
H|d \rangle = -\frac{\omega \hbar}{2}|d \rangle
$$

So the eigenvectors $|E_j \rangle$ are 
$\begin{pmatrix} 
1 \\
0
\end{pmatrix}
$
and 
$\begin{pmatrix} 
0 \\
1
\end{pmatrix}
$

To calculate the initial coefficients. For $|E_j \rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 
1 \\
0
\end{pmatrix}
$
$$
\alpha_j(0) = \langle E_j|\Psi(0) \rangle = 
\begin{pmatrix} 
1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} 
1 \\
0
\end{pmatrix}
=  1
$$

For $|E_j \rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 
0 \\
1
\end{pmatrix}
$
$$
\alpha_j(0) = \langle E_j|\Psi(0) \rangle = 
\begin{pmatrix} 
0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} 
1 \\
0
\end{pmatrix}
= 0
$$

So, 

$$|\Psi(t) \rangle = \alpha_j(0) \cdot e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}E_jt} |E_j \rangle = 1 \cdot e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\omega \hbar}{2}t}
\begin{pmatrix} 
1 \\
0
\end{pmatrix} =
e^{-\frac{i \omega t}{2}} |u \rangle
$$

For $\sigma_y$, the possible outcomes of the measurement (i.e. eigenvalues) are $\pm 1$, and eigenvectors are 

$$
|i \rangle = 
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\
\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}
\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
|o \rangle = 
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\
\frac{-i}{\sqrt{2}}
\end{pmatrix}
$$

The probability of each outcome is

$$
P(+1) = |\langle i|\Psi(t) \rangle|^2 = |e^{-\frac{i \omega t}{2}}|^2|\langle i|u \rangle|^2 = \frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
P(-1) = |\langle o|\Psi(t) \rangle|^2 = |e^{-\frac{i \omega t}{2}}|^2|\langle o|u \rangle|^2 = \frac{1}{2}
$$
