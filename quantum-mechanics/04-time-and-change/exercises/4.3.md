## Problem

Go back to the definition of Poisson brackets in Volume I and check that the identification in Eq. 4.21 is dimensionally consistent. Show that without the factor $\hbar$, it would not be.

$$
[F, G] \longleftrightarrow i \hbar \{F, G\}
$$

## Solution
From Volume 1,

$$
\{F, G\} = \Sigma_i(\frac{\partial F}{\partial q_i} \frac{\partial G}{\partial p_i} - \frac{\partial F}{\partial p_i} \frac{\partial G}{\partial q_i})
$$

So, the units of 

$$
[\{F, G\}] = \frac{[F][G]}{[q][p]}
$$

The product of $qp$ is action (momentum $\times$ length), which is also the unit of $\hbar$. Since $i$ is dimensionless, $i \hbar \{F, G\}$ has the unit of $FG$.

The commutator $[F, G] = FG - GF$ also has the unit of $FG$. Therefore, the identification is dimensionally consistent.
