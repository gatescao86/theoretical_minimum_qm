# Exercise 3.3 — Eigenvectors of $\hat{n}\cdot\sigma$

**Status.** Not started  
**Section.** 3.7

## Problem

Calculate the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of $\sigma_n$. Assume the eigenvectors $\lambda_1$ has the form
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 
cos \alpha \\
sin \alpha
\end{pmatrix}
$$

## Solution
First, find the eigenvalues by solving 
$$
\det(\sigma_n - \lambda I) = 0
$$
$$
\sigma_n - \lambda I = 
\begin{pmatrix} 
cos \theta - \lambda & sin \theta  \\
sin \theta & -cos \theta - \lambda
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\det(\sigma_n - \lambda I) = (cos \theta - \lambda)(-cos \theta - \lambda) - sin^2 \theta
= sin^2 \theta + cos^2 \theta - \lambda^2 = 0
$$
$$
\lambda^2 = sin^2 \theta + cos^2 \theta
$$
Since $sin^2 \theta + cos^2 \theta = 1$, $\lambda = \pm 1$

Then, for each eigenvalue $\lambda$, find the eigenvector by solving 
$$
(\sigma_n - \lambda I)
\begin{pmatrix} 
cos \alpha \\
sin \alpha
\end{pmatrix} = 
\begin{pmatrix} 
0 \\
0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
For $\lambda = 1$, 
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 
cos \theta - 1 & sin \theta  \\
sin \theta & -cos \theta - 1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} 
cos \alpha \\
sin \alpha
\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 
0 \\
0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
(cos \theta - 1)(cos \alpha) - sin \theta sin \alpha = 0
$$
$$
(cos \theta - 1)(cos \alpha) = sin \theta sin \alpha \\
$$
Divide both sides by $cos \alpha sin \theta$
$$
\frac{cos \theta - 1}{sin \theta} = tan \alpha
$$
Now use the half angle identity
$$
\frac{cos \theta - 1}{sin \theta} = \tan \frac{\theta}{2}
$$
$$
\alpha = \frac{\theta}{2}
$$
The eigenvector is 
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 
cos \frac{\theta}{2} \\
sin \frac{\theta}{2} 
\end{pmatrix}
$$
Similary, for $\lambda = -1$, the eigenvector is 
$$
\begin{pmatrix} 
-sin \frac{\theta}{2} \\
cos \frac{\theta}{2} 
\end{pmatrix}
$$
